A bit about me.

As any computational biologist, I have a set of "hammers" and "wrenches" that I like to use - such as machine learning, statistics, and graph algorithms - for my "nails" - such as genomics, transcriptomics, and proteomics. And, as any computational biologist, I always struggle to decide if I enjoy the computation more or the biology. This is a lovely problem to have.

I am aspiring to be a scientist and educator. I hope to be at the forefront of cutting-edge bioinformatics during my Ph.D., as I develop and apply computational methods to push forward our understanding of biology. The privilege of being affiliated with a medical center in New York City is that my work is grounded in healthcare applications. I also believe that science is about mentorship, teaching, and communication, as I exercise this muscle as a TA and student mentor.

My research: past, present, and future.

I started bioinformatics research during my undergraduate studies, working with leading epidemiologists, geneticists, ecologists, and microbiologists at Colgate University. It was a privilege to experience diverse flavors of biology and to address uniquely challenging needs of each scientist's respective specialty. In a similar fashion, during my Ph.D., I completed three research rotations with mentors working on orthogonal and fascinating biological problems. I invite you to learn more about my work.

To teach is to learn twice.

My scientific vision is complete with me in a classroom in some capacity. I have always taken immense joy in teaching and mentorship. There is something special about a student learning for the first time why the central limit theorem matters or how cells use crosstalk for complex signaling. As an advocate for personalized medicine in my research, I believe that there is seldom a one-cure-fits-all. The same applies to education. I live by the pedagogy of personalized education: catering to a student's interests, strengths, and needs. I embrace the use of technology and machine learning in my research and in my teaching - I am excited to see how technology enables us to make education more accessible, equitable, and impactful. But, I acknowledge that technology, when used incorrectly, can create inequitable barriers for learning. My teaching philosophy, like me, is a work in progress - I invite you to learn more.
